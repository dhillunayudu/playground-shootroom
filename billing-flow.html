The join is PlanPriceId, never StripePriceId

StripePriceId is a pointer out to the provider, not a join within the database. It is a VARCHAR with no foreign key, so joining on it gives no referential integrity — and if the provider ever changed, every such join would break. PlanId and PlanPriceId are yours.

Credits live on Plans, not on PlanPrices

Studio grants 600 credits whether it is paid monthly in rupees or annually in dollars. The entitlement belongs to the plan; the price belongs to the currency and interval. Putting MonthlyCredits on PlanPrices would mean maintaining the same 600 across four rows until one of them drifted.

Packs do not route through Plans

Both CreditPacks and PlanPrices hold a StripePriceId, but they point at different Stripe objects. There is no join between them. A pack is a transaction, not a relationship — the client's connection to it is historical, through the ledger entry and the invoice, not structural through a subscription.

Where both paths meetmoney goes to Invoices, credits go to the ledger

A payment produces two records that must not be confused. The invoice is what was billed and collected. The ledger entry is what the client can now spend. They are written by the same webhook and answer different questions.

Revenue never comes from SubscriptionPeriods

The relationship is many-to-one, not one-to-one. A period may have no invoice — a trial or a paused stretch. An invoice may have no period — a credit pack. And a mid-period upgrade settles two periods on one prorated invoice. Summing SubscriptionPeriods.TotalAmountMinor double-counts upgrades and omits packs entirely. Revenue is Invoices, always.

Ledger and balance are written together or not at all

A ledger row without a balance update leaves the cached number wrong. A balance update without a ledger row means credits moved with no explanation. One transaction, both writes, every time.

The monthly releasehow credits actually arrive

The job never counts rows or replays history. NextCreditReleaseAt is a counter on the subscription; the job seeks on it and advances it.

Find what is due
WHERE Status = 'active' AND NextCreditReleaseAt <= NOW() — a single index seek. Ten thousand subscriptions return only the handful due right now. Paused and past-due subscriptions are excluded: no payment, no credits.
Compute the carry-forward
Carry is min(current balance, Plans.RolloverCapCredits). Anything above the cap is written as its own RolloverCapped ledger entry, so the customer sees what lapsed rather than watching a number drop. Purchased pack credits are excluded from the cap — they were paid for separately.
Grant the allowance
A PlanRelease entry for Plans.MonthlyCredits. Expiry is written before the grant, so the balance is never briefly over the cap.
Advance the counter
NextCreditReleaseAt += 1 month, and on an annual plan ReleasesRemaining -= 1. Idempotent on subscription and release month, so a retried job cannot double-credit.

Why the two dates are separate

The billing period and the credit release schedule are different things. An annual customer pays once and receives credits twelve times. Collapsing them into one field would either hand over a year of compute on day one, or starve an annual customer for eleven months.

A renewal moves CurrentPeriodStart and CurrentPeriodEnd forward on the existing row and writes a new SubscriptionPeriods child. An upgrade mid-period closes the current child as superseded and opens a new one, so the record of what was charged survives the change.

Worked question"which invoice covers August for this client?"

Not by filtering invoices on date. An invoice raised on 12 August covers 12 Aug to 12 Sep — partly August, partly September — and a credit pack bought on 20 August produces an August invoice that is not a subscription invoice at all.

Find the period that spans the month
SubscriptionPeriods WHERE ClientId = ? AND PeriodStart <= '2026-08-31' AND PeriodEnd >= '2026-08-01'
Read its InvoiceId
That is the subscription invoice covering August. Null means the period was never invoiced — a trial, or a paused stretch.
For everything billed in August instead
Invoices WHERE ClientId = ? AND IssuedAt IN August — this includes credit packs and seat prorations, which belong to no period. A different question, and the only correct one for revenue.

Reading this laterthe three rules worth remembering

One. Join on your own keys — PlanId, PlanPriceId. Provider identifiers point outward only.

Two. Entitlements sit on Plans; prices sit on PlanPrices. Never duplicate an entitlement across price rows.

Three. The ledger is the authority. CreditBalances is a cache and UsageSnapshots is a summary; both are rebuildable, neither is truth.
